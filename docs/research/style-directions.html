Four style directions, measured against the current build

The current site is a clean, well-made dev-portfolio rail. The problem is the skeleton, not the polish. This page shows four replacements, rendered as real CSS in this file. Each one reuses your existing content.

Why the current page reads as mainstream

  • The skeleton is a 2015 template. Sticky rail + avatar + name + numbered nav (01-04) + timeline + rounded card grid + pill tags. Delete the pixel accents and it is indistinguishable from any Astro or Framer theme.
  • Identity lives in the decoration. The pixel layer is corner ticks, dashed rules, and a bot on the avatar. It is paint over a neutral grid, so the personality stops at the surface.
  • The numbers do not pay off. "100+ engineers mentored" sits in a stat card. The evidence behind it is a text list. A hiring manager reads the claim and never sees the artifact.
  • One layout for one audience. Recruiter, engineer lead, and client all get the same scroll. Nothing is fast for the skimmer and deep for the reader.

Sources: Why 90% of developer portfolios look the same, Your portfolio should look like you, Why every design portfolio looks the same, The case against portfolio templates.

Direction 01

Operators desk - a portfolio that boots

highest identity, highest build cost

The whole site becomes one working desktop. The rail is gone. Navigation is a menu bar and a taskbar, sections open as windows, and your CV is a draggable stack of documents. The pixel work you already own becomes the design system instead of an accent, and the terminal answers real commands against your own data.

Precedent: ZezinOS 98, Retro OS portfolio, KABIR-OS, Abhishek Jha (SOTD, Tetris easter egg). Trend risk: browsers-as-OS is now a named genre, so the differentiator must be your real artifacts, not the nostalgia.

Wins

  • Maximum memorability. Nobody forgets a portfolio that boots.
  • Reuses the pixel layer you already built, as the system instead of the trim.
  • Memorable for engineering leads. Signals craft and restraint under constraints.

Costs and risks

  • Genre risk: browsers-as-OS is a crowded trend of its own now.
  • Mobile needs a real plan. Drag and hover do not exist on touch.
  • Accessibility must be rebuilt: focus order, keyboard window management, reduced motion.
  • Highest build cost of the four.
Direction 02

Swiss dossier - evidence over decoration

best hiring signal, lowest risk

No cards, no pills, no rounded corners. A strict two-rail grid, a numbered index of work, and a fact table that prints fine at A4. Every claim carries its artifact: metric, period, and proof link. The design statement is the restraint, and the page proves engineering discipline more than it describes it.

Precedent: Swiss editorial direction in the Style and Aesthetic hub, varied-layout portfolio gallery, Nahel Moussi (SOTD, single type size).

Wins

  • Fastest to scan. A recruiter gets the claim and the proof in one line.
  • Lowest risk: no metaphor to explain, prints and exports cleanly.
  • Works on any device with one grid, because there is no spatial model to break.

Costs and risks

  • Less playful. It competes on taste, not on spectacle.
  • Needs real proof links per claim, or the table looks like bravado.
  • Retires most of the pixel layer. That work moves to the detail pages, or stops.
Direction 03

Zine press - print, not screen

highest memorability per pixel

The page becomes a printed object: two spot inks on uncoated stock, a real serif for headlines, mono for data, grain and slight misregistration instead of gradients. The pixel portrait becomes a coarse halftone plate. Your CV reads as a spread in a small-run magazine about one engineer.

Precedent: risograph design reference, retro futuristic UI kit, Inkpress Atelier zine brief, PX PUSH on a simulated CRT.

Wins

  • Feels authored. Print logic resists the sameness that template logic creates.
  • Keeps your pixel portrait, recast as a halftone plate.
  • Cheap in bandwidth: type, two inks, one image.

Costs and risks

  • Must be executed hard. A half-printed look reads as a mistake.
  • Ink contrast needs an audit in both themes for WCAG AA.
  • Signals design over engineering. Pair it with one technical proof section.
Direction 04

Build status - the proof is the product

strongest senior signal, needs real data

The page is a live control panel of your engineering practice: deploy status, commit activity, uptime of the projects you run, and mentoring throughput as a real time series. It answers the one question a skeptical lead has: does this person actually operate software, or only describe it. Built at build time, so it stays static and cheap.

Precedent: retro terminal and telemetry components, industrial brutalist and CRT telemetry direction, how hiring managers evaluate portfolios, evaluate a portfolio in 10 checks.

Wins

  • Answers the senior question directly: this person runs software.
  • Distinctive without nostalgia. Nothing else on the market looks like it.
  • Numbers become live evidence, not a stat card.

Costs and risks

  • Needs real data sources. Faked telemetry is worse than none.
  • Empty or stale panels look bad. Define the fallback state first.
  • Cold and impersonal unless a human section sits beside it.
